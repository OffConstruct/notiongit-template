A commonplace book is a notebook of things worth keeping. My class keeps
one together: questions, facts and drawings.[^1] Here is how it works.

## The rules

1. Write the date before anything else.
2. Copy the source with every note, even a page number.
3. Never delete an entry; cross it out instead.

**Question** - any pupil can add one, with their initials.

**Answer** - added later, by anyone who finds it.

## A worked example

| Question | Answered by | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why is the sea salty? | MK, Year 6 | with a diagram |
| Do fish sleep? | the whole class | still arguing |
| How deep is the harbour? | the harbour master | 6 metres |

> A good question is worth more than a quick answer.

When the notes moved to a computer I wrote a tiny script to rebuild the index:

```text
Why is the sea salty?      answered  p. 4
Do fish sleep?             open      p. 9
How deep is the harbour?   answered  p. 12
```

[^1]: The first book is now in the school library.
