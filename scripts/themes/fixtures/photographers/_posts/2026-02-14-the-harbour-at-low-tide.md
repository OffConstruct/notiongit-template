Twice a day the water leaves, and the harbour shows you its bones: the chains,
the old tyres hung as fenders, the steps worn into a bowl by two hundred years
of boots. For six winters I stood at the same wall at the lowest water of each
month and made one exposure.

## What the mud keeps

A spoon, a bicycle bell, the brass plate from a boat that sank before anyone
now living was born. The mud keeps everything and gives it back slowly.

> The harbour is not a place. It is a schedule that happens to have walls.

![The pier at dusk, low water]({{FIXTURES}}/photo-shore-32.jpg)

*March. The old pier at dusk, the last frame on the roll.*

The book collects forty-eight of these plates, one for each month, in the
order they were made.
